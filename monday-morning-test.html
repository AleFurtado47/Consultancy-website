What the five show.

What these five mechanisms are, and why they sit in two groups

Five mechanisms decide whether an approved priority changes anything on Monday. They are not a sequence, and neither group depends on the other, so an organisation can be sound on one and stuck on the other.

The first group asks whether the strategy can command the work: authorship and the budget both feed ownership, because someone can only own an outcome if they shaped the work and can pay for it. The second asks whether the organisation can see the work moving and steer it, where reporting hands off to deciding and what matters is the quality of what passes between them.

What five answers cannot see

One person answered. The Review puts the same questions to around fifteen people across every layer, and to the records the organisation already holds.

How hard the evidence is to get. That is a reading in its own right, and this page cannot produce it. A mechanism that looks sound but takes two weeks and a working group to evidence is not yet a system.

Where people disagree. You have put one mark on what is believed. The Review sets a whole layer's marks against what the documents show, and that distance is usually where the value sits. Case study 02 is what it looks like when the two come apart.

This reading reflects your assessment of how work happens in your organisation. It identifies patterns in your answers; a full diagnosis would also examine organisational records and the perspectives of other people involved.

Where to start

One first move per constraint, in the order the mechanisms appear above. Which order they actually need to run in is one of the things the Review establishes.
